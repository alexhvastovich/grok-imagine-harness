# Cinematic staging before frame generation

Before generating a scene pair, lock a small set plan and camera plan. A style
reference alone does not establish spatial continuity. Record actor/furniture
positions, lens, camera height, shot size, action axis and the intended cut.

## Composition and spatial acceptance

- Use one coherent horizon and vanishing system for floor, bench and shelving.
- Relate body scale to depth, door height and bench height. Same-depth adults
  should have comparable head sizes. Seated bodies still occupy the same floor.
- Separate foreground, action plane and background. A shelf edge must not imply
  an actor is squeezed beside a cabinet when their feet imply another depth.
- Keep hands, dropped objects and the important doorway legible. Avoid crowded
  silhouettes, tangencies, clipped shoes, and an unexplained oversized foreground.
- Choose shot size for the beat: readable two-shot for setup, entrance coverage,
  eye insert for panic, close-up for the final declaration. Establish wide before
  close coverage. Use deliberate smash cuts for the anime joke.
- Preserve the action axis and screen direction: assistant left, coworker right,
  doorway further right. Their looks connect. Cross the axis only deliberately.
- Lock a camera for continuous A→B→C. Do not move shelving, alter door proportions,
  or change perspective between endpoints. Generate B using the inspected A.
- Inspect clothing construction, hands, prop ownership, eye direction and anatomy
  as well as faces. A visually broken frame fails even if a file/hash exists.

## Physical and environmental continuity

For action endpoints, lock the stationary objects as well as the characters.
Compare the silhouette, feet/base, scale and position of cover, machinery,
columns and cables. A different camera position needs coherent parallax; it must
not be achieved by sliding a wall across a fixed view. A wall used as cover is
not a door. Specify which object moves and which objects remain fixed.

A sprint should begin in a credible gait phase with a supporting/pushing boot,
weight over the planted leg and a contact shadow or splash. Two feet in the air
can be a real running phase, but the Marine example encouraged floating between
endpoints. Review successive foot contacts and stationary floor references in
the motion; speeding up skating does not fix it.

Check gaze in three dimensions: seeing a face aimed screen-left is insufficient
when the target is away down in a valley. A rear three-quarter observer, with
head and foreshortened rifle directed toward the visible factory, makes that
relation legible. Use the same observation axis in the aftermath.

Judge projectile scale against nearby same-depth architecture, not the empty
sky. Record body size separately from flame/smoke size and compare both endpoints.
The Marine revision's first vertical warhead was building-sized; it was rejected
and reduced substantially. This is a visual scale correction, not a measured
real-world weapon dimension. For a descending missile, the nose is below the
tail and exhaust; trajectory, body scale and exhaust direction must remain
consistent throughout, including the final frame.

Track persistent effects explicitly. If the factory is burning before a Ghost
cloaks, the clean endpoint must still show the same burning wreckage. Cloaking
changes only the character and carried equipment; it must not dissolve the
whole scene, extinguish fires, stop smoke or restore intact buildings. Inspect
background motion through the effect, not only its first/last stills.


### Check the following shot after a trajectory repair
A corrected approach can still fail if the impact shot retains its old smoke trail. In Ghost Strike,13 was vertical but14 still showed a diagonal approach. Inspect the last approach frames and first impact frames together; repair the dependent endpoint and take as well. Compare projectile body against fixed architecture, separately from exhaust and smoke. Do not call a visual estimate a canon scale measurement.
