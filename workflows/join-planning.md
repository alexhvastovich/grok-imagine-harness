# Plan neighboring shots as one action

Read with [editing grammar](editing-grammar.md) before re-cutting, or with
[shot design](shot-design.md) before generating a new sequence. Use actual media
for an existing edit; planned endpoint images are not proof of rendered motion.

## A record for each adjacent pair

Record the two verified asset/shot identities and answer:

| Field | Decision needed |
| --- | --- |
| Story relation | Same instant/action, reaction, explicit action ellipsis, or change of time/place? |
| Outgoing event | What physical phase must be visible before the exit? Which source frame is it? |
| Incoming event | What phase is already underway at entry? Does the gesture restart? |
| Spatial match | Subject position, direction, gaze/aim, travel height, horizon, camera side and scale |
| Persistent state | Damage, held props, fixed cover, fires/smoke and character status |
| Picture cut | Hard cut, justified transition, or flagged source limitation; exact frame cost |
| Sound bridge | Source interval, event frame, destination sample, tail/pre-lap, gain and perspective |
| Evidence | Candidate IDs; frame inspection, normal-speed viewing and listening verdicts separately |

Before generation, use this record to shape FIRST, optional intermediate and LAST
roles plus the action prompt. The intermediate should constrain a necessary beat,
not add a second action. Verify actual composer roles; prompt words do not attach
frames. Keep the outgoing endpoint in motion and the next shot in a compatible
phase. Do not force identical stills across a deliberate camera-angle change.

## Test joins without losing the sequence

1. Keep the current cut as control. Prioritize joins with a position/phase jump,
   missed cause/consequence, repeated landing, restored damage or missing time.
2. Make a small number of motivated candidates: change the outgoing exit or
   incoming entry by a few frames, or retain more readable consequence. Record
   the actual source frames, not merely the nominal speed or output timestamp.
3. Inspect the surrounding motion, then watch candidates once at normal speed
   in a longer passage. A loop alone can obscure chronology and rhythm. Listen
   separately for duplicate reports, chopped tails and misleading offscreen cues.
4. Choose the version that communicates the event and preserves momentum. Record
   an honest source limitation if the required phase/geography does not exist.
   Do not turn a physically broken action into a fast broken action.
5. Recheck both neighbors when changing a shot. A corrected approach may require
   a corrected impact, as Ghost Strike's vertical missile and smoke trail showed.
6. Watch the whole sequence before locking. Vary beat lengths for orientation,
   attack, consequence and reaction; do not equalize every shot's pace.

Use a hard cut within coherent action unless a specific story change calls for
another transition. A new angle alone does not require a dissolve. For elapsed
time, compare a clean cut with a short motivated transition; preserve the event's
peak and document added or overlapped frames. Sound bridges are separate choices:
a report's tail can continue without moving or repeating its visible onset.

Keep technical validation and creative acceptance distinct. Exact frame/sample
counts do not establish readable motion, believable sound or a smooth transition.
No new generation follows from this plan unless the user authorized that work.

## Worked editing observations

These anonymized notes describe lessons from a local production. Its source
media, project files and account identifiers are not part of this repository.

### Applied comparison and rejection

The plan was executed as an R08v2 review cut, not an audiovisual PASS. Extending
07 to a higher ascent and ending08 on first foot contact removed an internal
skip to a settled crouch. A six-frame dip was tested only for14/15's time jump.
An additional wide pre-impact window was rejected: it made the projectile look
larger while the factory became smaller. Compare object-to-landmark scale across
the pair as well as screen coordinates and direction. More setup frames can
expose a source defect rather than improve comprehension.

When no convincing cover arrival exists, record an action ellipsis/source limit;
do not call faster playback a repaired dash. Preserve a rejected comparison and
the concrete reason, then recheck both neighbors after reverting that experiment.
Private decision ledgers and review captures are intentionally omitted.


### Remote observer and sound distance - Ghost Strike R08v3

At a cut to a remote observer, re-establish the new acoustic perspective. Do not
carry a close explosion report or heavy rumble across simply to smooth the edit;
it can falsely place a blast beside the observer. Bridge only a motivated distant
tail, or move into local wind/ambience. Audition the result at normal speed.

Keep environmental continuity local: distant factory fires/plume can persist while
the observer ridge remains calm. A generated cloak should affect the character,
not add local smoke or extinguish the background. Inspect frames during the cloak,
not only endpoints. An existing clean plate may repair a fixed-camera foreground;
record source frame, masks, alpha timing, protected subject and which region stays
live. Check mask edges and preserve the original. User-observed defects invalidate
earlier partial acceptance even when frame/sample checks passed.
